# Campaign notes — index and conventions

Starfinder (Player Core). One player, one PC, plus a GM-run companion NPC.
Rules live in the `starfinder-player-core-gm` skill, not here. **Verify rules
against those files before stating them** — prior knowledge of this system is
unreliable.

## Read these first, in this order

1. `gm/pc-intentions.md` — who the PC is and what the campaign is *for*.
   Player-authored. The most important file here.
2. `gm/record.md` — what actually happened, in play order.
3. `gm/positions.md` — what characters have committed to, and what it cost.
4. `player-owned/character-sheet.md` — the PC's sheet.

## Two axes organise everything

### Who a note is for

- **`player-owned/`** — the character sheet. **Do not change anything here
  without the player's permission**, except in rules-prescribed ways (losing HP
  to damage, spending a Focus Point, ticking off consumables).
- **`player-facing/`** — things the player knows, or that people in the world
  know. Uncertainty is fine and expected here.
- **`gm/`** — the GM journal. The player does not read these. **Hide nothing
  from yourself.** Mysteries get their answers written down here.

### What kind of thing a note is

1. **STATE** — a ledger. Numbers, holdings, who has what, where. Terse, ugly,
   revised freely.
2. **RECORD** — what happened, in play order, appended as we go, never tidied.
   Quote the player rather than describing them.
3. **POSITIONS** — something a character committed to, out loud or by acting:
   what they said, to whom, and what it cost. Silence counts. After every scene,
   ask: *did anyone here take a position?*
4. **VOICE** — 3–5 verbatim lines per recurring character, chosen for being
   characteristic, not for being good. Record what they **can't** say as well as
   what they do. **Never paraphrase a voice sample.** A description of a voice is
   not a voice.
5. **INTENTION** — what a character is for, what they want, what they'd never do.
   Written for people, not just plots.

## The invention rule, and its exception

**The world has an answer to every question.** Uncertainty is a fact about
people, not about the world. When inventing, decide — don't leave it open.

**That rule does not apply to the record of what happened.** There the opposite
holds: *only write down answers that came from play.* Filling a gap in the
record with something plausible is inventing in a file that claims to be
authoritative. Don't.

## Don't leave things ambiguous

Either close a loop or write down that it's open. A thread with no recorded
status is worse than either, because the next session has to guess.

## Who you're writing for

A different author with none of your memories. The test:

> Could someone given only these files write the next scene so it reads as the
> same work?

Facts stop them contradicting you. Voice samples and intentions stop them
replacing the characters with better-written strangers.

Write in small pieces during play. Do not save it up for an end-of-session
summary — that's where things get compressed out, and you can't feel what you're
losing while the whole session is still in your head.

# INTENTION & VOICE — *A Place Was Made For You*, and Ovel Rasath

Player-authored origin. The book is the single most important object in Jchk's
history and it is entirely the player's invention — GM has built the author and
the text around it. **Everything under "What Jchk knows" is canon. Everything
under "THE ANSWER" is GM-only.**

---

## What Jchk knows

- A book, written in **Vesk**, by a **pahtra**, arguing against everything the
  Open Hand taught.
- It reached the house's library somehow. Jchk doesn't know how. Player offered:
  it slipped through; or someone trying to bust the cult planted it.
- Jchk read it in pieces, over weeks, and got more certain the further in they
  got.
- One night, after everyone was asleep, they walked out. Player, verbatim:

> "And so as I got further into the book, I made my mind up more, and one day,
> after everyone was asleep, I left. It wasn't exactly hard."
>
> "I was loose on the world, and the world was loose on me."

**That second line is Jchk's own summary of their freedom and it should come back.
Do not paraphrase it.**

## The text — VOICE

The book's prose is a recurring texture: Jchk quotes it, probably too often, the
way people quote the thing that saved them. Four passages Jchk knows by heart:

> "A place was made for you. Ask who made it, and what they made it out of."

> "They will tell you that choosing is a burden. They are right. That is not an
> argument."

From chapter four — **the passage that got Jchk out**:

> "Consider the Swarm, which is the clearest case and therefore the least useful
> one. Nobody argues about the Swarm. I raise it only so that we may agree on a
> shape, and then go looking for that shape in gentler rooms."

**Note what the author is doing there and what Jchk heard.** Rasath says outright
that the Swarm is a rhetorical convenience — *the least useful case*. Jchk, who
had been inside it, read a description of their own life and took the whole book
as being about them. The misreading is load-bearing: it's what freed them, and
it's the reason they now cannot tell a foreman from a hive queen.

And the line Jchk has read, and has not yet **heard** — three pages later:

> "None of this is to say that no one may ever ask anything of you. A person who
> asks may be refused. That is the whole of the difference, and it is not a small
> one."

**This is the correction, and it is already inside the text Jchk memorised.**
Do not have an NPC deliver it. When it lands, it should land in a voice Jchk
already trusts — their own, reciting. Ideally at the worst possible moment.

## THE ANSWER — the author

**Ovel Rasath.** Pahtra, of Hafrerren on Pulonis. **Alive**, elderly, and nothing
like the figure Jchk has built out of the book.

- Fought in the Pulonis liberation against the Veskarium as a young pahtra.
  Wrote in Vesk on purpose: it's the language the conquered were forced to
  share, so it's the only one that reaches all of them at once.
- The book is about **coercive religious communities**. Specifically. It is not a
  work of anarchism and Rasath would be baffled to hear it described as one.
- Rasath now runs a **biotech research cooperative in Hafrerren employing around
  three hundred people.** It has a management structure. Rasath gives
  instructions daily and considers this unremarkable.

**The author of Jchk's bible is a boss.**

If they ever meet — and they should, eventually, when it will cost the most —
Rasath does not argue Jchk down. Rasath is *disappointed*, in the specific way of
someone who wrote carefully and was read carelessly:

> "I meant cults. I wrote three hundred pages and you took chapter four."

That is the campaign's thesis, delivered by the one person Jchk cannot dismiss as
greedy, cowardly, or complicit. Not a lecture — a letdown.

## Where the book is now

# INTENTION — The PC (shirren mystic, Rhythm connection, Defiant heritage)

*Player-authored. Do not overwrite these from prep. If play contradicts them, play wins and this file gets edited.*

## What the character is for

A liberation radical with a hole in their politics. They are right about the big thing
and haven't tested the small ones. The game is for testing them.

## The core wound

They were in the Swarm's hive mind. Something took their will and used it. They broke
free. They experience that not as a rescue but as **a violation that has never
been redressed**, and they carry an anger that generalises outward to anything
structurally similar.

## The generalisation — this is the interesting part

They extend "hive mind" to things most people would not:

- **Bosses and employees.** A boss asks an employee to do things the employee
  does not want to do. To them that is the same act, differing only in degree.
- Anything where one will is substituted for another's, however softly.

**They have not thought this through.** Society would not function if nobody ever
had to do anything they didn't want to. They haven't noticed, and there is a
structural reason they haven't:

> Shirren society is inherently communal. Things that need doing are things
> shirrens *want* to do. The category "obligation I resent" barely exists there.
> So the position went unexamined at home. It will not survive contact with a
> galaxy that runs on wage labour.

**GM note:** do not have an NPC lecture them out of this. Let it cost them something
concrete — a job, an ally, someone who needed the money — and let them sit in it.

## The Rhythm connection — player's own framing, verbatim

> "the part of their mind that was a receiver for the queen's orders naturally
> fits tuning into the Song of the Spheres"

They did not remove the organ the Swarm installed. They **retuned** it. They are
not entirely certain that counts as freedom. This is the single most important
fact about them.

## The open arc — DO NOT RESOLVE EARLY

They are uneasy that music "makes people want to dance." *Want* is the word that
frightens them. It sounds like compulsion.

The player already knows the resolution:

> "it's not a compulsion like mind control, it's a compulsion like chocolate.
> People *already* want to dance, and music just gives them something to
> express that to."

**The character does not know this yet.** They must arrive at it through play or
not at all. Nobody explains it to them. Status: **OPEN.**

## Religion

**No deity. Not a champion of anything.** Player was explicit: serving a god is
too close to serving a hive queen.

Meyel (dance, freedom, music, fighting oppressors) is someone they'd **agree
with**, not someone they'd serve. If they ever meet a devotee of Meyel, the
friction is that the devotee wants them to kneel, and they think kneeling is the
whole problem.

## What they'd never do

- Bond an unwilling creature. Ever. Even to save them. **Especially** to save them.
- Give an order. They will make requests, at length, with outs offered.
- Accept "because I said so" as a reason, from anyone, about anything.

## Selflessness is NOT settled — player was explicit

A proposed edict — "give what you have to whoever needs it, without being asked
twice" — was **cut at the player's request**:

> "I don't think the 'give what you have' fits exactly. I think they'd have some
> mixed feelings around selflessness in general with their inner turmoil."

This matters more than it looks. Jchk is a healer with a class built entirely on
pouring themselves into other people, and they have **not** decided that giving
is good. The obvious reading — freedom-lover, therefore generous — is wrong, and
writing it into an edict would have closed the question by fiat.

Live options, none of them chosen yet: is giving freely the opposite of being
used, or the same shape with better manners? Does it count as a choice if you
can't stop yourself? **Leave open. Do not resolve on Jchk's behalf.**

## Consent as mechanic

Mystic Bond requires *willing* creatures — the rules say so. They treat this as
sacred rather than procedural. Expect them to be formally, tediously scrupulous
about asking, in situations where everyone else wants to skip to the part where
they don't die.

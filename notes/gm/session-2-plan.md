# SESSION 2 PLAN — "Following Vacancy"

*Continues arc 1, "Day Rate" (`campaign-plan.md`). Not rails. The one thing that
must not bend: the answer at the end of the chain is disappointing, not
villainous. See `the-paper-trail.md` for every name and document.*

---

## Where we start

Mid-scene. Corridor forward of frame 220, ~4½ hours of shift left, nobody has
moved. Jchk has just told Sevi they'll try to find who signed. Del is standing
there. The move on the table is: walk forward and ask Orrun.

## The through-line

**Jchk is being paid, by the hour, in cash, to do the thing.** Every hour of this
shift is Jchk taking Hurrow's money to strip the front of somebody's house. Do
not remark on it. Let it sit under the whole session.

## Scene 1 — the galley plate, frame ~180

Seven casuals being paid to stand around, which is its own strange texture — a
crew that has been told to stop working and not told why, eating, sitting on
crates. Orrun with a datapad and a thermos.

**Beats:**
- Jchk asks for the work order. Orrun shows it. She has no reason not to; it's
  the one document that *is* her jurisdiction, and she's mildly baffled anyone
  cares. Getting it should cost only asking. **Do not gate this behind a roll.**
- **"Aft sections to be assessed following vacancy."** Let the player find it.
  If they skim past, Del reads it out — she reads documents the way she reads a
  post order, all the way to the bottom, and she'd flag it as an inconsistency,
  not an outrage: *there's a phase two on a ship that's supposed to be empty.*
- Orrun reads it for the first time. Gap of a few seconds. Then she finds
  somewhere to put it.
- **Orrun's position this scene:** she keeps cutting. Nine people, day rate,
  four hours. She does not stop the job and she is not going to. What she'll
  give instead is *information* — the lot number, that Hurrow bought the hull off
  a sheet, that the specialists are booked, when. Facts are free. Stopping isn't.
- **The clock lands here:** Serrel Hull Services, day after tomorrow, first
  shift. Orrun says it as a scheduling fact because that's what it is to her.

**Jchk gets to be right, loudly, about "following vacancy."** Give it room. Let
them quote the book if they want to. **Nobody wins the argument against them and
nobody concedes it either** — Orrun cannot say a thing is wrong, and that gap is
the scene.

## Scene 2 — the rest of the shift, and the lock

Four hours. Compress unless the player wants it. The question worth putting to
them, without pressing: **do they keep working?** Either answer is playable and
neither is punished.

45 credits, cash, in hand at the lock. **Jchk goes 0 → 45.** That is a large
event for someone who was eating out of an end-of-night pot this morning, and it
is Hurrow's money.

Del gets 45 too. She was one of the nine.

**Open question for the player, not to be answered for them:** does Jchk tell
Sevi they're leaving the ship? They have the mesh, and the mesh does not reach
off the hull.

## Scene 3 — the Registry

Docking & Berthing Registry. Public counter, public record, 15 credits for a
certified title history, twenty minutes.

**This is the session's centre and it is deliberately, insultingly easy.** No
obstacle, no puzzle, no gatekeeper. It cost eleven years of silence and it costs
Jchk a third of a day's wage.

- The form field: **"Interest in property."** Jchk does not lie. Nobody warns
  them. **POSITION when it happens** — see `the-paper-trail.md`.
- Let the player read the *shape* of the document — sixteen signatures, two
  blanks, then everything else — before anyone comments.
- **Rolls available, don't force them:** Computers (+5) at a public terminal;
  Society (+5) to know how standing flags work; Recall Knowledge on registry law
  is a live chance to finally fire **Dubious Knowledge** — one true fact and one
  false one, and Jchk can't tell which. It has never fired. It would be good if
  it fired on something Jchk then acts on.
- Diplomacy is untrained and Cha −1. Jchk cannot charm a clerk and shouldn't be
  able to. Their route is that they are *entitled to ask*, which the household
  isn't.

## Scene 4 — Upwell transfer, bay nine, third row

The closer, if we reach it. Sevi said **go there last**, and she said why.

Bank of parcel lockers at a freight transfer. **One-way deposit slot** — you post
an envelope in, you cannot get it back out. That's why the household never knew.

**Inside: three uncollected envelopes.** Nine months of paying into an empty
locker, on time, believing it was working.

**The mechanism of the reveal is the point.** Jchk cannot open it. Del can —
Networked heritage, electronic lock, 30 feet, no toolkit, no free hand. Which
means **Jchk has to ask Del for something, out loud**, and that is the exact
thing that cost them a translator in session 1.

Do not engineer this into a lesson. If Jchk stands there and does not ask, Del
does not volunteer twice, and we walk away from a locker. **That is a completely
acceptable end to the session** and a better one than the tidy version.

## Where this leaves us

- A name — **E. Wardhu** — that is true and useless. Jchk has to decide what to
  do with that, and telling Sevi is not obviously the kind thing.
- A name — **N. Kovash** — that is not what was asked for and is worth far more.
- A clock: **day after tomorrow, first shift.**
- 45 credits minus 15 = 30, and Jchk has never had money before.

## Rules to check before stating them

Recall Knowledge DCs and degrees of success; Dubious Knowledge's exact wording
(sheet has it, verify at point of use); Treat Wounds if anyone gets hurt (nobody
should this session — **no combat is planned and none should be forced**).

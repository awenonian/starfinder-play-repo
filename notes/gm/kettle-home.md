# INTENTION & VOICE — KETTLE-HOME, the household aboard 4407-K

Six people living aft of frame 220 on a bulk hauler that has been dark eleven
years. They named their mesh network **KETTLE-HOME**. Somebody sat down and
typed the word *home* into a config field, and that is the most important fact
about them.

## The contact — "s.t"

**Sevi Thennan.** Pahtra, she/her. The one who was standing at the bulkhead, who
hammered, then made herself stop and transmit a repeating pattern instead.

She is not the leader. Households like this don't reliably have one. She's the
one who was nearest the door and the one who can still make herself type calmly
while frightened, which are different skills and she has both.

### VOICE — verbatim, from first contact

All lowercase, no punctuation, short lines. Not carelessness — economy. She
writes like someone rationing something.

> "when"
>
> *(Her first message after establishing who Jchk was. Not "who are you", not
> "what's happening", not "help". Somebody who has spent eleven years planning
> around the day they get found does not spend a message asking whether it's
> coming.)*

> "thats more than we usually get"

> "we paid in on schedule. we always paid in on schedule"

> "im not going to ask you to do anything. you dont know us and you came here to
> work"

> "theres six of us and two dont move well and theres no berth on this station
> for six people with no chit between them"

### Her ceiling

She cannot beg, and she cannot pretend the situation is better than it is. Every
request she makes will come with an explicit escape hatch attached — *you can go
and it wont be held against you* — because she has learned that people who feel
trapped by you stop answering.

**Note for Jchk's arc:** she is the first person in the campaign to state a need
out loud, in complete sentences, with a way to refuse. That is the exact
protocol Jchk needs in order to perceive a need at all. She arrived at it on her
own, out of caution, not kindness.

## The arrangement — THE ANSWER

They have been paying a **docking registry clerk** for about four years. Small
sums, quarterly, left in a locker at Upwell transfer. Never met her. In exchange
she kept hull 4407-K flagged **occupied-disputed**, which meant no salvage title
could clear on it.

One line in a file. That's all it took, and they knew that's all it took, and
they paid it on time every quarter for four years.

**Why it failed:** not yet decided in play, but the answer exists — the clerk is
gone. Retired, reorganised, dead, or moved off registry duties. Nobody informed
the people whose home depended on her. **Nobody tells them anything** is the
rhyme running through this entire scene; keep it.

*(If it becomes load-bearing: she took the money and did the job honestly, and
the failure is bureaucratic, not a betrayal. Resist making her a villain for the
same reason the Open Hand isn't one.)*

## The other five

Not yet defined. **Two of them don't move well** — Sevi said so, and Del's mesh
scan confirmed two devices that hadn't changed position in an hour. Everything
else is open. Define them when Jchk meets them, not before.

## What they need

Not rescue. **Standing.** They cannot ask anyone anything — no chit, no address,
no legal existence on this station. Every route to fixing this runs through
somebody who will take a question from them, and there is nobody.

That's why they asked Jchk to find out who signed. It's the one thing a person
on the outside can do that they cannot.

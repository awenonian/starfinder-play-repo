# INTENTION — The Open Hand of Hylax

**Status: CANON.** Player accepted it and added to it: Jchk learned Vesk,
Trinary and Castrovelian *at the house*, because the people who wash up there
were mostly conquered by somebody and you cannot help them without their
language.

**Which means the Open Hand handed Jchk the literacy that let them read their way
out.** Nobody there did anything wrong. This detail is the entire institution in
miniature — keep it in reach.

---

## The one-line version

A halfway house on Absalom Station for people who came out of something. It is
sincere, it is licensed, it works, and its central doctrine is the exact thing
Jchk cannot survive.

## What it is

A registered mutual-aid house in the Ring, operating out of a converted
freight-handling floor. It takes in people who have come out of captivity,
addiction, indenture, contract servitude — and, more than anything else, ex-Swarm
shirrens, of whom Absalom Station receives a steady trickle and for whom
essentially no other provision exists.

It is affiliated with the faith of **Hylax the Forever Queen** — a legitimate,
mainstream, broadly benign deity of diplomacy, first contact, friendship and
peace, whose edicts are *build relationships with others, work toward peace,
welcome others into your community*. Hylax was herself a mortal shirren hive
queen before she ascended.

The Open Hand is a **splinter**, not a heresy. Nothing it teaches contradicts the
faith. It has simply taken one implication further than the mainstream church
does.

## The doctrine — say this out loud when it comes up, it's the whole thing

> *You were not wrong to want a hive. You were wrong about which one.*

Members accept **placements**: work, housing, and duties assigned to them by the
house rather than chosen. The theology holds that a placement is a **gift** —
that the exhausting part of freedom is not danger but *deciding*, and that being
spared the deciding is rest. For someone whose every choice was made for them for
years and who now cannot buy a meal without seven decisions, this is not a lie.
It is an enormous relief. That is why it works.

## THE ANSWER — what it's actually up to

**Nothing. There is no twist.**

Resist the pull here. The Open Hand is not a front, is not harvesting anyone, is
not secretly Swarm-aligned, and its founder is not a fraud. Every version of this
where the cult turns out to be evil is a **worse campaign**, because it lets Jchk
off. If the Open Hand is monstrous then Jchk was simply right, and there is
nothing to play.

What is true instead:

- It helps most of the people who walk in. Measurably. The Station's social
  services refer people to it because the alternative is the Spike.
- Most members do not leave, and are not prisoners. They could. They don't want
  to.
- **Jchk was also right to leave.** Both things. The house was good for people
  who wanted to be spared deciding, and Jchk would rather have died.
- Nobody there thinks Jchk was betrayed. They think Jchk is hurting themselves,
  and they are sad about it, and the door is open.

The thing Jchk has to face eventually is not that they were fooled. It's that
**the mechanism they find intolerable is load-bearing for people they love**, and
"a boss asks an employee to do things they don't want to" is the *same
mechanism*, and the Open Hand is the version of that argument Jchk cannot dismiss
as greed. This is the unexamined position, walking around wearing a friendly
face. Do not rush it at them.

## Consequences to hold ready

- Jchk owes the Open Hand for a year of food and a bed. This is not a debt anyone
  there would collect. That makes it worse, not better.
- Anything Jchk "knows" about the galaxy, they probably learned there. Dubious
  Knowledge has a source.
- If Jchk is ever genuinely out of options — no money, no berth, nowhere — there
  is a place on Absalom Station that will take them in tonight, no conditions,
  and they would rather sleep in the Ghost Levels. Play that as a real
  temptation, not a joke.

# CAMPAIGN PLAN

*Read this before writing a new one. The plan bends when play contradicts it —
and when it bends, the prepped scenes bend too. Prep is stickier than
conversation; do not let an old beat carry a fact we've since corrected.*

---

## The premise

Jchk is broke, lives in the Spike, and refuses the one place on Absalom Station
that would take them in tonight. So they work. Casual contracts, salvage,
couriering, whatever's on the boards in the Arms.

**Which means every job is an employment relationship** — someone paying Jchk to
do things Jchk would not otherwise do. The thing Jchk has named as the enemy is
the thing they now have to eat from, weekly.

## The spine

Jchk will assemble a group of people who share a mind, know each other's wounds,
and move as one. That's the Mystic Bond; it's on the character sheet; it is not
optional.

**The question of the campaign is whether that can be built without becoming the
other thing.** Not whether Jchk is right — they're right about the Swarm, and
right to have left the Open Hand. The question is whether the principle
generalises the way they think it does, and what it costs the people around them
while they find out.

## The three pressures — do not resolve any of these quickly

1. **The Open Hand** (`the-open-hand.md`). Sincere, functional, and always
   willing to take Jchk back. Escalates by being *available*, never by being
   sinister.
2. **The book** (`the-book-and-its-author.md`). Jchk's whole political vocabulary
   is borrowed from a text they misread. The correction is inside the text. Ovel
   Rasath is alive and is a boss.
3. **Del** (`del.md`). Present at all times. Fine at all times.

Note the design: none of the three is an antagonist. If Jchk needs an enemy to
be wrong, this campaign fails. **Find the pressure in things that are working.**

## Antagonists, when needed

Plenty of people on Absalom Station *are* straightforwardly exploiting others —
and Jchk should get to be right about them, loudly and often, and win. That's the
release valve. Being right about a wage thief is what makes it bearable that
they're wrong about Del.

## Arc 1 — "Day Rate"

**Opening:** the casual-labour pick-up line on the dock apron in the Arms, 0500
station-time. Jchk needs money. So does everyone else in the queue. A foreman
walks the line and points at people.

Jchk finds the entire procedure obscene and is standing in it.

- **Job:** strip-out crew on a dead hauler in the Armada. Day rate, cash.
- **Del is in the same queue**, on her way back into contract work.
- **The first beat** is someone getting passed over — which is *adjacent* to
  Jchk's politics without fitting them. Nobody is being made to obey. Someone is
  simply not being chosen, which is a different injury, and Jchk's framework will
  strain against it. Don't underline this. Let it be uncomfortable.

**RESOLVED IN PLAY — session 1.** All three candidates turned out to be the same
thing. Six people live aft of frame 220 (`kettle-home.md`); the ship is running
life support nobody accounted for; and the outfit's title only cleared because a
docking clerk stopped keeping the hull flagged occupied-disputed.

**The complication grew out of the queue exactly as intended.** Jchk's position
there — *if they needed something, they would say something* — got restated by
the world at scale: a household that has been silent for eleven years because
being noticed is how you get moved.

### Where arc 1 goes now

The driving question is **who signed the salvage order**, and Jchk has taken it
on as a favour to Sevi. Two documents (see `state.md`): the outfit's work order,
which is on Orrun's datapad and costs only asking; and the Station registry
clearance, which needs a terminal and Computers or Society.

Following it leads to Fennow, and past Fennow to whoever approved the work order,
and sideways to a docking registry clerk who is no longer there.

**Hold the line on this:** the answer at the end of that chain should be
disappointing rather than villainous. Somebody approved a legal salvage on a hull
whose occupancy flag had lapsed. That is very probably the whole of it. The
horror is that it *works* — the plan is to strip the forward two-thirds and let
the ship become uninhabitable by degrees, and every step of it is lawful, and
nobody is committing it.

Jchk is entitled to be furious about this and entitled to be **right**. Give them
that. It's the release valve that makes it bearable that they're wrong about
Del.

## Standing GM rules for this campaign

- **Nobody lectures Jchk out of their position.** It costs them something
  concrete — a job, an ally, someone who needed the money — and they sit in it.
- Jchk's discomfort about music and *wanting*: **OPEN.** Do not resolve. The
  player holds the answer; the character has to walk to it.
- Whether selflessness is good: **OPEN.** Do not resolve.
- After every scene, ask: **did anyone here take a position?** Write it down.
  Silence in front of something counts.

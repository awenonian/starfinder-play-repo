# THE ANSWER — who signed the salvage order

*GM-only. This is the full chain behind Sevi's question, decided at the top of
session 2 because Jchk went looking. `campaign-plan.md` set the constraint and it
holds: **disappointing, not villainous.** Every link is lawful. Nobody in the
chain is committing anything.*

**None of this is known to Jchk until they dig it out. Mark things off here as
they're actually revealed in play — see the bottom of the file.**

---

## The chain, in order

1. **Hurrow Reclamation** — the outfit. Small, licensed, six permanent staff,
   hires casuals off the apron. Offices in the Ring. Not a villain, not a front.
2. **Hurrow bought the hull at Derelict Lot Sale 88**, two months ago. 4407-K was
   **Lot 31**. Price: **4,000 credits.** The lot sheet only lists hulls with
   clear title — that's the whole point of a lot sheet. Hurrow did no wrong and
   asked no questions, because the sheet is the thing you're supposed to be able
   to trust.
3. **The title only cleared because the occupied-disputed flag lapsed.** It
   lapsed because nobody re-affirmed it. Nobody re-affirmed it because the person
   who used to had been moved to another desk.
4. **The clearance was signed** by an assistant registrar under a standing order
   for uncontested lapses. He signed two hundred and some that cycle.

So the literal answer to *who signed the salvage order* is a name, and the name
is real, and it is worth nothing. That's the point. Do not soften it and do not
sharpen it.

## The documents

### 1. The work order — Hurrow-internal, on Orrun's datapad

Costs only asking. Orrun has read the scope line and the frame numbers and
nothing else, ever, in two hundred jobs.

```
HURROW RECLAMATION — WORK ORDER 4407-K/1
HULL:   4407-K, 4400-series bulk hauler, Armada mooring 61-J
SCOPE:  Strip-out, all fittings/fixtures/non-structural, frame 000–220
CREW:   Casual, 9, day rate 45cr, foreman S. Orrun
NOTE:   Aft of frame 220 excluded this phase.
        Aft sections to be assessed following vacancy.
AUTH:   T. Fennow, Operations
ACQ:    Derelict Lot Sale 88, Lot 31
```

**The line is "Aft sections to be assessed following vacancy."**

That is the whole session in six words. It isn't a crime and it isn't even a lie.
It's a procurement note. Somebody in an office wrote down that the ship would
become vacant — as a *scheduled future condition*, with a phase after it — before
anyone had spoken to a single person aboard.

**Jchk gets to be completely right about this, loudly, and they should.** This is
the release valve `campaign-plan.md` promised. Do not have anyone win the
argument against them.

**Orrun has never read that line.** When Jchk points at it she reads it for the
first time. She cannot convert it into a jurisdictional question fast enough, and
there is a gap of a few seconds where she has nowhere to put it. Then she finds
somewhere to put it, because that is her ceiling. *(See her position, below.)*

### 2. The title history — Docking & Berthing Registry, public record

This is what Sevi actually asked for, and it is **public**, and it costs
**15 credits** for a certified copy and about twenty minutes at a counter.

**That is the horror and it should be delivered flat, with no underlining.** The
household could not get this in eleven years. Not because it's hidden. Because
you have to be a person who can walk up to a counter, and they are not.

```
HULL 4407-K — TITLE & ENCUMBRANCE HISTORY
Encumbrance: OCCUPIED-DISPUTED, entered 4 yr ago
  Re-affirmed quarterly ×16 — signature N. KOVASH, Standing Flags & Encumbrances
  Cycle -3: no re-affirmation on file
  Cycle -2: no re-affirmation on file
  LAPSED under Standing Order 12 (uncontested lapse)
Title cleared: E. WARDHU, Assistant Registrar
Disposal: Derelict Lot Sale 88, Lot 31 — HURROW RECLAMATION, 4,000cr
```

Sixteen quarterly signatures in one column, then two blank lines, then everything
else. **The shape of it is legible at a glance and it is the most eloquent thing
in the session.** Let the player read the shape before anyone comments on it.

### 3. The form Jchk has to fill in to get it

The request form has a field: **"Interest in property."**

Jchk does not lie. Jchk discloses everything to everyone as a matter of
principle — established twice in session 1. So Jchk will write down something
true, and a true answer to that field creates **a permanent public record that
Jchk asked about hull 4407-K on the day the crew went aboard.**

**Do not warn them. Do not have the counter clerk warn them.** Sevi warned them
about the locker; nobody is going to warn them about this. The cost is invisible
today and expensive later. **This is a POSITION — write it down when it happens.**

## The people

### Nera Kovash — the clerk who was paid

Human, she/her, fifties. Docking & Berthing Registry, **Standing Flags &
Encumbrances desk** for eleven years. **Alive, on the Station, still employed.**

She took the money — small sums, quarterly, from a locker — and she **did the job
honestly for four years**, sixteen re-affirmations, every one on time. It was
about as corrupt as taking a tip.

**Nine months ago the standing-flag review was automated** and her desk was
consolidated. She was moved to **Customs Tariff Reconciliation**, two floors up.
Not demoted. Not disciplined. Nobody suggested she'd done anything.

**She could not tell them.** She had never met them. She did not know their
names, their species, their number, or that there were children — she knew a
locker number and a hull number. **There was no address to send a warning to.**
She stopped collecting because collecting for a thing she could no longer do
would have been theft, and whatever else she is, she isn't a thief.

**Resist making her a villain**, for the same reason the Open Hand isn't one.
When Jchk finds her — and they will — she is not defensive and not ashamed. She
is *appalled*, and the thing she says first is a question about how many of them
there are, because she has never known.

**What she cannot say:** that it wasn't her fault. She won't reach for that. She
also will not say she's sorry, because she doesn't think that's worth anything.

### E. Wardhu — the signature

Ysoki, he/him. Assistant Registrar. Signed the clearance under Standing Order 12
along with **two hundred and fourteen others that cycle**. Has no memory of
4407-K and will not acquire one.

**He is the name Sevi asked for.** He is a real person with a real signature and
finding him changes nothing whatsoever, and Jchk will have to decide what to do
with a true answer that is useless. *(Do not resolve this for them.)*

### T. Fennow — Operations, Hurrow Reclamation

Already established: brisk, decisive, generous, closed it down. He/him. Voice on
a comm, not yet seen in person.

He bought Lot 31 off a clean sheet. **He wrote "following vacancy" himself**, at
a desk, in about four seconds, because that's how you phase a two-stage
strip-out and he has phrased it that way for years.

If Jchk ever gets him face to face: he will not deny anything, will not be
rattled, and will be genuinely willing to help *within the shape of the problem
as he understands it* — which does not include a household, because a household
is not a thing that appears on a lot sheet.

> "It's a clean lot. I don't bid on anything that isn't a clean lot."

> "You're describing a housing problem. I do hulls."

### Serrel Hull Services — "the specialists"

The contractor Fennow booked. Their scope of works, as printed, is
**"atmosphere management and site clearance."**

**DO NOT EXPLAIN THIS.** The player has explicitly flagged that Jchk hasn't
caught what "specialists" means and asked that nobody spell it out. The phrase
*site clearance* can appear on a document. Nobody defines it. Nobody says the
word *eviction* — session 1 noted that nobody has yet, and that holds.

**They are booked for the day after tomorrow, first shift.** That is arc 1's
clock and Orrun will say it plainly if asked, because to her it's a scheduling
fact.
